My story

I don't want one road. I want the map.

I've wanted to be a thinker for as long as I can remember. Not just someone who thinks about things, either. I want to try the idea and see what happens. The Level Everything Company is where those ideas finally get somewhere to go.

I'm Alex Shade. I live in Southern Indiana, and I run The Level Everything Company.

You can look at an app or a photo I made and see the finished thing. What you don't see is everything before it: the question that got stuck in my head, the attempts that didn't work, the late night when it finally clicked.

That's the part I want you to know about. It's the part I care about most.

It started with a Windows 95 computer.

When I was about ten, I was already digging around in Windows 95-era computers, looking at the hardware and trying to figure out what made them tick. The computers have changed a lot since then. I haven't.

I spend a lot of time outside, too. Hiking, exploring old historic places, wandering through small towns, collecting firewood. I almost always come home with a new question.

And I like making things with my hands. I made a wooden handle for a fire poker. I turned a pile of old bricks into a little outdoor fireplace. I added lighting to make an outdoor space nicer to sit in. I mess around with a Dremel, with wood-burning, and with old or odd equipment just to see what it can do.

None of that makes me a tradesman. I do it because I love it. I'm planning to post photos and notes on these projects the same way I do for the software.

If I'm curious about something, I end up doing something about it.

How an idea turns into something

It usually starts with a question. Could this tool do something it wasn't made for? Could I build something that fixes this annoying problem? What happens if I put these two ideas together?

Then comes the hard part. An idea needs a first try. The first try needs testing. Sometimes the test sends me right back to the start. That's fine. Honestly, that's most of the fun.

I'm still learning, and I'd rather be upfront about that. Everything I put out says what works and what doesn't yet.

The experience behind the ideas

Before any of this, I spent about 11 years working with people: sales floors, a wireless store, local businesses, radio ads. Most of the job was listening, figuring out what someone actually needed, and explaining things so they made sense. I still work that way.

Sales & customer relationships

I’ve helped people work through their options, ask questions, and find something that makes sense for them. It starts with listening.

Working with businesses

I’ve sat down with business owners, learned what they were trying to accomplish, and helped connect their needs with practical ideas.

Radio & digital advertising

I’ve helped local businesses get their message out through radio and digital ads, writing copy, contributing to recordings, and helping shape campaigns.

Community & business events

I’ve met local business owners through Chamber and community events—getting out there, having conversations, and learning what people are building.

Phones & mobile technology

I’ve sold phones and service plans, explained features, and helped people figure out why their technology wasn’t doing what they needed.

Store leadership

I’ve managed a wireless store, supported the people working beside me, handled customer concerns, and kept daily operations moving.

Retail experience

I’ve worked with everything from phones to jewelry and mattresses. Different products, different people, and plenty of practice finding the right questions to ask.

Everyday problem-solving

I’ve worked through frustrated conversations, confusing technology, and problems that needed an answer. I’ve learned to explain things clearly and keep looking when the first answer doesn’t work.

My independent work

Outside of work, I take photos, build software with help from AI, run AI on my own equipment, and try to see what I can get out of the gear I already have. I come up with an idea, try it, find what breaks, and try again. That's where most of The Level Everything Company comes from.

Things I keep coming back to

Southern Indiana Lore: I keep wondering what stories are sitting around us that nobody notices. That question turned into an app for local history and folklore you can use on your phone or in a browser.

Math Garden: My kids were learning math, so I turned it into a game. Earn coins, grow a garden, watch your progress. Sometimes making something means making a better way to learn.

Our family display and calendar: I want technology to actually be useful at home. Keeping track of plans, and finding things for us to go do together.

Local AI and my own tools: I want to know what I can do with the equipment I already have, and build a way of working that fits how my brain works.

Photography, scanners, machines, and VR: I'm always looking for something new hiding inside something familiar. Sometimes that turns into a photo. Sometimes it turns into a tool.

Math Garden and the family tools are just for us for now, so you won't find them as public downloads.

How I use AI

I use AI to research, work through ideas, write code, and dig into problems. I bring the questions and decide what's worth building. AI helps me find ways to get there, and I keep checking the result against what I actually wanted.

A program that builds isn't the same as a program that works. That's why every public project lists what it needs, what's been tested, and what hasn't.

Why Level Everything?

For a while my work was split between Wommper1, Alex Shade Photography, and Alex Shade Productions. But my interests kept spilling into each other, and I wanted one place for all of it.

Photography changes how I tell a story. A story can pull me into local history. Local history turned into an app. Level Everything is the name for that whole chain.

What I really want is a life where thinking, making, and learning have a real place. This company is how I'm building toward it.
