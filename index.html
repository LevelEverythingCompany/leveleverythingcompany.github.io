Local history

Southern Indiana Lore

Android release · Web beta

Southern Indiana is full of history and folklore most people drive right past. Lore points it out, and every story shows its source and how solid the evidence is.

Runs on: Android 7+; browser edition for iPhone and desktop. Android tested on an S25 Ultra; other devices remain untested.

Try the web atlas ↗Download Android APK ↗
Source & instructions ↗Report a bug ↗
Inside the build & known limits

Why I built it: I kept wondering what stories were sitting in the places I pass every day. I wanted a phone that would tell me.

What it does: Offline site data, location alerts, narration, and a browser atlas.

What's still rough: GPS alerts are an Android feature. The web atlas needs to stay open for Near me. The map is schematic, and some historical details remain unverified.

WaveForgeVideo

Windows release

Trim, convert, rotate, or resize a video without opening a full editing program for a two-minute job.

Runs on: Developed and tested on Windows 11. Install FFmpeg separately; the Windows ZIP does not need Python.

Download Windows ZIP ↗
Source & instructions ↗Report a bug ↗
Inside the build & known limits

Why I built it: Sometimes I just need part of a clip, or the same clip in a different format. That shouldn't take a whole editing project.

What it does: A desktop front end for FFmpeg, with trim controls, previews, conversion, and export logging.

What's still rough: One clip at a time. Previews are still frames; there is no multi-clip timeline. Trimming re-encodes video.

Why I built it: I wanted the same sound playing on two Bluetooth devices at once, and Windows doesn't make that easy.

Early release · Experimental VR features

Live captions and English translations of what people are saying in VRChat, running on your own Windows PC.

Runs on: Windows 11 x64, Visual C++ 2015–2022 x64 Redistributable, and an initial speech-model download. CPU processing.

Inside the build & known limits

Why I built it: VRChat puts you in a room with people from all over the world. I wanted a way to follow along when they aren't speaking English.

What it does: Local speech recognition, translated captions, desktop overlays, and an optional local phone display.

AI radio

Local Talk Radio

Windows release · v1.2.0

Two AI hosts talk to each other, and the station turns the conversation into a finished radio show with voices, music beds, station idents, commercial breaks and a timecoded transcript. It all runs on your own computer, with no account and no subscription.

TWO AI HOSTS · ON AIRTwo hosts. One show.Voices · music beds · ad breaks
Transcript · podcast feedConcept diagram, not an app screenshot.

Runs on: Developed and tested on Windows; the Windows ZIP includes Node.js. Needs a local model server such as LM Studio or Ollama and an instruct model of about 8B; 6 GB of VRAM is enough. From source, the server also runs on Linux; macOS is untested.

Download Windows ZIP ↗
Source & instructions ↗Report a bug ↗
Inside the build & known limits

Why I built it: I spent years helping local businesses get on the radio, writing ad copy and helping with recordings. I wanted to see if the gear I already have could run a whole station by itself, from the hosts all the way to the commercial breaks.

What it does: Two separate AI hosts take turns answering each other, and you hear each line a few seconds after it's written. You can call in by typing a name and a line, hosts can refer back to earlier shows, and the Podcast tab publishes the shows you pick as MP3s with a podcast feed and a small website.

What's still rough: The download isn't code-signed, so Windows may warn you; choose More info → Run anyway. The optional Edge voices send each line's text to Microsoft and use an unofficial service that may change. Some Piper voices don't allow commercial use, so check a voice's license before publishing shows. The station has no login, so keep it on your own PC and never expose it to a network.

Work with me

Got something you want made?

I spent about 11 years in sales, running a wireless store, and helping local businesses with radio and digital ads. Most of that job was figuring out what people actually needed. Now I do the same thing with photos, websites, small tools, and AI. I'm a freelancer in Southern Indiana, and I work remote or in person.

01

Photography & creative media

Photos, editing, and creative content. Tell me what you want to capture, where it's going to end up, and the look you're after.

02

Websites & software projects

A website, a small tool, or a rough first version of an idea. Show me the job you want to make easier and what "done" looks like to you.

03

Practical AI & technology ideas

Got a computer task you do over and over? Show me the steps. If a small tool or some automation can take it off your plate, we'll figure that out together. And if you've got a strange idea you want to try, I'm interested.

1 / Send me a message

What you're trying to do, what you already have, a rough budget, and when you need it. If it's in person, tell me what town.

2 / We talk it over

We figure out whether I'm the right fit and what a sensible first step looks like. I check my schedule before I promise anything.

3 / We agree before I start

What you get, the price, the timing, and how revisions work are all settled up front. Sending a message doesn't commit you to anything.
