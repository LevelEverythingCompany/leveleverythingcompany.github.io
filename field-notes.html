Field note 004 / November 25, 2026

My website sounded like a robot.

I read my own homepage this week and something bugged me. It said I “turn questions into software, creative tools, and experiments.” It had slogans like “Imagine. Build. Test. Keep learning.” It called me a visionary three different times.

None of it was wrong, exactly. It just didn't sound like me. People online have a name for this now: AI slop. It's writing that's smooth and polished but could be about anybody.

That's a little funny, because I use AI to help build almost everything here. AI didn't cause the problem. I let it do the talking. So I went back through every page with it and asked one question: would I actually say this out loud? If the answer was no, it got rewritten.

Now the homepage starts with me at ten, poking around inside a Windows 95 computer. The story page talks about the brick fireplace and the fire-poker handle instead of “turning a thought into something real.” The slogans are gone. I also made the pages load faster and made the menu the same on every page, which nobody will notice, which is the point.

What I learned: AI is great at getting something built. It's not great at being me. That part is still my job.

What's next: December 23 is the big reveal, when Wommper1, Alex Shade Photography, and Alex Shade Productions all become The Level Everything Company. Between now and then I'm adding real photos of my work. If something on the site still sounds like a robot, tell me.

Field note 003 / October 25, 2026

One clip. One useful result.

Here's the kind of question I like: can a job I do all the time take fewer steps? WaveForgeVideo is my answer for video. Here's how it works, start to finish.

I wrote this from the user guide and a real screenshot. It isn't a recording of a new test run.

1. The question

Sometimes I just want to keep part of a clip, rotate it, or convert it. That shouldn't take a full editing project. WaveForgeVideo puts those controls in one window.

2. The build

Under the hood it's FFmpeg, a free tool that does the heavy video work. My app puts buttons on top of it: source info, trim points, still-frame previews, output presets, and an export log. The export is saved as a separate file from your original.

3. Try a small example

  1. Use a copy of a short, non-private video that is longer than 20 seconds. Install FFmpeg separately and confirm the app finds it.
  2. Load the clip. For this example, set Trim start to 12.5 seconds and Trim end to 20 seconds.
  3. Check the START and END still frames. They show trim points; they are not video playback.
  4. Choose MP4 H.264 and Keep source resolution. Use Choose Output + Start and choose a new output filename.
  5. Open the finished file in a media player. Check that it contains the intended segment and that its picture and sound are as expected.

What you should get: a clip about 7.5 seconds long. That's what those settings should produce. I didn't re-time it for this note.

4. What's still rough

FFmpeg must be installed separately. Previews are still pictures. Trimming re-encodes the video and takes export time. This is a single-clip tool; it does not have a multi-clip editing timeline.

According to the guide, if a hardware export fails, the app falls back to software encoding, and every export gets its own log. When something goes wrong, you can see why.

5. Tell me how it went

Could you find the trim controls? Did you get what you expected? Did you know what you needed before you downloaded it? Tell me where you got stuck. That's exactly what I need to make it better.

Field note 001 / A new beginning

Why this place exists.

I used to think each new interest needed its own name and its own place. One for photos. Another for stories. One for coding. Another for an experiment that started at midnight and got completely out of control.

Now I'd rather have one home where the threads are allowed to cross.

That's what these field notes are for. Not every idea will be a masterpiece. But the process deserves a record: what I tried, what broke, what surprised me, and what someone else might be able to use.

If a question feels too weird to belong here, it's probably exactly the kind of question I want to investigate.

The WaveForgeVideo walkthrough above starts that record with a real interface, a documented task, and the limits that matter when trying it.
